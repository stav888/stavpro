Cleopatra lived closer to the Moon landing than to the building of this pyramid.
Around 2560 BC, on the Giza plateau, Egypt raised a tomb for the pharaoh Khufu.
It rose nearly one hundred and forty-seven meters, built from about two point three million stone blocks.
For almost four thousand years, it was the tallest structure made by human hands.
Its sides face north, south, east, and west, aligned with astonishing precision.
And it did not look like this. It was sheathed in polished white limestone that blazed beneath the desert sun.
The builders were not slaves. They were skilled, paid workers, who were buried with honor in tombs beside the king.
Of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, only one still stands.
This one.
