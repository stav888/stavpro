Over two thousand years ago, in the desert, the Nabataeans carved a city into rose-red cliffs.
Its gateway is the Siq, a dark, narrow crack winding nearly a mile through the rock.
The light fades. Footsteps echo.
And then, like a dream, the Treasury appears, carved straight into the cliff.
But this was only the beginning.
Behind it stretched an entire city, with temples, royal tombs, and a theater carved into the stone.
A brilliant water system turned the dry desert into a blooming oasis.
Camel caravans brought incense, spices, and riches from every corner of the world.
Then earthquakes and new trade routes slowly made the world forget it.
For centuries, Petra vanished from the map.
Until it was found again, a hidden rose-red city, waiting quietly to be remembered.
