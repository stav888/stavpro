On-screen title: THE SOLDIER WHO FOUGHT FOR 29 YEARS

Narration (10 parts):

1. For 29 years, one soldier refused to believe World War II was over. Why did he keep fighting for so long?
2. In December 1944, Hiroo Onoda, a Japanese army intelligence officer, was sent to Lubang Island in the Philippines, near the end of the war.
3. His orders were clear. He was never to surrender.
4. Japan surrendered in 1945. But Onoda and a few of his men stayed hidden in the jungle.
5. Leaflets announcing the end of the war reached them. They dismissed every one as an enemy trick.
6. Over the years, his companions were lost. Akatsu left in 1950. Shimada was killed in 1954. Kozuka was killed in 1972.
7. In February 1974, a young Japanese adventurer named Norio Suzuki found him still hiding in the jungle.
8. Onoda refused to give up. He would surrender only if ordered by his commanding officer.
9. In March 1974, his former commander, Major Yoshimi Taniguchi, flew to Lubang and formally relieved him of duty.
10. Onoda surrendered his sword to Philippine President Marcos, who pardoned him. For 29 years, he had kept fighting a war that was already over.
