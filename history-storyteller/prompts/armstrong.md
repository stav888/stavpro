## Script / narration
Final English script:

### `script_en.txt`

Source: `history-storyteller/examples/armstrong/script_en.txt`

```
In 1933, inventor Edwin Armstrong patented FM, a radio signal free of static. He showed it to his old friend David Sarnoff, the head of RCA, America's radio giant. RCA let him test FM from the top of the Empire State Building, and the sound was crystal clear. But Sarnoff was betting RCA's future on television, and he had Armstrong's equipment removed. Armstrong spent his own fortune building FM stations anyway. Then in 1945, the government moved FM to a new part of the dial, and every FM radio already sold became useless. RCA used FM in its new TV sets without paying him, so Armstrong sued. The court battle dragged on for years and drained his money and his strength. In 1954, exhausted, Armstrong took his own life. His wife Marion kept fighting, and in the end she won or settled every case. Today, FM is in almost every radio on Earth. 
```
