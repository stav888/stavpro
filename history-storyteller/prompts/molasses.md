On a bright winter day in 1919, a giant tank stood over Boston's harbor.
It held over two million gallons of sticky molasses.
For months it leaked and groaned, and the company just painted it brown to hide the leaks.
Just after noon came a sound like gunfire, as rivets flew and the tank burst open.
A brown wave twenty-five feet high raced through the streets, crushing buildings and even bending the railway.
The cold made the molasses thick like glue, trapping people and horses.
Twenty-one people died, and sailors and firefighters worked for days as the harbor turned brown.
In court, it came out the tank was never properly tested, leading to stricter engineering rules.
And for decades, on hot days, the streets still smelled sweet.
