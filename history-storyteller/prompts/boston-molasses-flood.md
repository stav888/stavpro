Narration (10 parts):

1. A wave of molasses 25 feet high tore through a city. How could something so sweet turn so deadly?
2. In 1919, a giant tank stood on Commercial Street, in Boston's North End. It belonged to Purity Distilling, owned by United States Industrial Alcohol.
3. Inside were about 2.3 million gallons of molasses, meant for industrial alcohol.
4. The tank was poorly built. It leaked for months. The company just painted it brown to hide the stains.
5. Around midday, rivets shot out like gunfire. The tank burst open.
6. A brown wave, 25 feet high, raced through the streets at about 35 miles an hour, crushing buildings and bending the railway.
7. Then the cold made the molasses thick like glue, trapping people and horses in the streets.
8. Twenty-one people were killed, and about 150 were injured. Rescuers searched for days as the harbor turned brown.
9. In court, the company blamed saboteurs. An auditor found the tank was never properly built. United States Industrial Alcohol was found liable.
10. For decades, people said the North End still smelled of molasses on hot days. A wave of molasses 25 feet high had torn through a city.
