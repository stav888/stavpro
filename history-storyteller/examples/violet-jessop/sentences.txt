In 1911, Violet Jessop was a stewardess on the Olympic, the largest ship in the world.
That autumn, the warship HMS Hawke rammed its side, but Violet walked away unhurt.
Months later, she joined the Olympic's new sister ship, the Titanic.
In April 1912, it struck an iceberg, and Violet was sent into lifeboat sixteen with a baby in her arms.
On the rescue ship Carpathia, a woman took the baby back without a single word.
During World War One, she became a nurse on the third sister, the hospital ship Britannic.
In 1916, Britannic hit a mine, and Violet's lifeboat was pulled toward the spinning propellers.
She jumped, struck her head under the water, and still survived.
She kept working at sea for decades, and people called her Miss Unsinkable.
